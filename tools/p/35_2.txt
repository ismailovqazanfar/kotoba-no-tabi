Title: The Little Bakery's Important Guest

In a town, there was a little bakery. Its name was Morning Flower. The owner, Mika, got up early every morning and baked bread. Although the shop was small, it always smelled wonderful.

One day, the shop received a phone call. A woman said, “A famous cooking teacher will visit your bakery tomorrow.” Mika was surprised and asked, “Around what time will the teacher be coming?” The woman replied, “At ten in the morning.” Mika's heart began to race.

The next morning, Mika cleaned the shop carefully. She baked plenty of fresh bread, too. She put a sign saying “Welcome” at the entrance. Then, in front of a mirror, she practiced polite expressions such as “Please enjoy this” and “I shall bring you some tea.”

Ten o'clock came, but the teacher did not arrive. At half past ten, a little old woman entered the shop. She wore old clothes and carried a large bag. “One of these breads, please,” the old woman said. Mika smiled and replied, “Certainly.”

The old woman tasted the bread and said, “It's very delicious.” Mika replied, “Thank you very much. Shall I bring you some tea, too?” The old woman said, “Yes, please.” They chatted quietly near the window. The old woman asked many questions about how the bread was made.

By noon, the famous teacher still had not arrived. Mika felt a little disappointed. Then the old woman took a photograph from her bag. It showed her younger self standing in front of a large cooking school. The old woman laughed and said, “Actually, I am that teacher.”

Mika was astonished and quickly bowed. “I didn't know who you were. Please forgive me,” she said humbly. But the old woman smiled kindly. “Even though you didn't know who I was, you treated me with kindness. That made me happiest of all,” she said. Mika blushed.

A few days later, Mika's bakery was featured in the town newspaper. The article called it “The Warmest Bakery in the World.” From then on, many customers visited every day. Mika welcomed everyone by saying, “Welcome. What would you like to eat today?” And that same old woman still came in asking for “my usual bread.”
