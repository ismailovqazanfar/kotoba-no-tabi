Title: The Bunbuku Tea Kettle

Once upon a time, an old man who sold old things lived in a town. One day, he found a tanuki caught in a trap beside the road. He freed the poor creature and went home.

That night, the tanuki came to the old man's house. “This is to thank you,” it said, and turned into a shiny tea kettle. A tea kettle was a vessel for heating water for tea, and this one had little feet.

In the morning, a monk from the temple came by. “Is this a tea kettle or a toy?” he asked. When the old man answered, “A tea kettle,” the monk said, “I see. I'll take this kettle, please.”

At the temple, the monk put the kettle over the fire. Suddenly, a face and feet popped out of it. “Hot! Hot!” cried the kettle, and the startled monk exclaimed, “What is this?”

The kettle ran to the old man's house. “Is that the temple's kettle?” a customer asked. “Yes, it is. But that kettle is my friend,” the old man answered, and opened the door.

“Heating water is no good for me. But I can dance,” said the tanuki. The next day, a long rope was stretched across the garden. In its kettle shape, the tanuki walked along the rope and danced merrily.

Many people came to watch. “Is that umbrella yours?” a child asked the old man. “No, it isn't. It's the tanuki's,” he answered, and the tanuki opened the umbrella and took a bow.

That evening, the old man heated water in another kettle. “Is that one a friend of mine?” asked the tanuki. When it heard, “No, this is an ordinary kettle,” the tanuki relaxed and drank its tea.
