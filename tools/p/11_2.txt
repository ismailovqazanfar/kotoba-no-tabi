Title: Just One Stamp

A boy named Haru lived in a small town. Once a month, he wrote a letter to his grandmother. Her house was about two hours away by train.

On Saturday around nine, Haru wrote a letter. On the paper, he drew three cats and five flowers. There was only one stamp in the box on his desk.

His little sister Mei wrote to their grandmother, too. On Mei's paper were six apples and seven whole mandarins. They each took an envelope, but there was just one stamp.

The post office was about fifteen minutes away on foot. The two children went there with their mother. “Just one stamp, please.” Haru took some money from his pocket.

The postal clerk looked at the two letters. “They're going to the same house. Do you have a large envelope?” Their mother had one in her bag. The children put both letters inside it.

The clerk checked the envelope's weight. “That one stamp you have will be enough.” Haru did not buy a new stamp. On the way home, the children bought one ice cream each.

Four days later, around five in the evening, a parcel arrived from their grandmother. Inside were six apples and seven whole mandarins. There was a little letter, too.

“Thank you for the shopping list. Three cats won't fit in the box. I'll bring just the five flowers next week.” Their mother read the letter aloud. The children looked at each other and burst out laughing.
