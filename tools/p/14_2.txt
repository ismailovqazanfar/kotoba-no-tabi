Title: To Midori-cho, Please

On Saturday morning, Mr. Tanaka, a taxi driver, was waiting for passengers outside the station. It was his birthday. But his family had been busy all morning and had said nothing about it. He felt a little lonely.

Just then, an old woman arrived carrying a large box. “Excuse me, please open the door.” Mr. Tanaka got out and opened it. “Shall I carry that box?” he asked.

The woman was holding the box carefully. “Thank you, but I’m all right. To Midori-cho, please.” Then she showed him a little piece of paper. “Please look at this map.”

The taxi left the station and drove along the river. Something was rustling inside the box. “Are you hot? Shall I open the window?” Mr. Tanaka asked. “No, please leave it as it is.”

At the next corner, the woman said, “Please turn right.” The road ahead was near Mr. Tanaka’s home. “Please stop in front of that white house.” The white house was his own.

Surprised, Mr. Tanaka stopped the taxi. “Excuse me, please give me a little help.” Together, they carried the box to the front door. Inside, the house was dark and very quiet.

“Please turn on the light,” said the woman. The room lit up, and his family shouted, “Happy birthday!” The woman opened the box. Colorful balloons floated out.

The woman was his wife’s friend. His wife had asked her, “Please go and bring my husband home.” His daughter asked, “Shall I make some tea?” Mr. Tanaka smiled and said, “Yes. And today’s passenger, please sit with us too.”
