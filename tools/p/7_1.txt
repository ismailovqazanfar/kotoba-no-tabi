Title: The Post Office That Delivered to the Past

One rainy day, Yui entered an old post office. The hands of the clock were turning backward. “We send letters to the past from here,” said the man at the counter.

That morning, Yui had received an old photograph from her grandfather. In the picture, he was seven years old and alone. “My friends did not come, even on my birthday,” he had said.

Yui received some paper from the man. “Any pencils?” she asked. He lent her a pencil and an eraser. Yui drew a little cake with the pencil.

“What is ‘omedetou’ in English?” Yui asked. The man taught her “congratulations.” Yui wrote the long word, but now the paper was full.

Yui received another sheet of paper. She also asked, “What is ‘tomodachi’ in English?” She learned “friend” from the man. This time, she wrote in Japanese, “I am your friend.”

The man taught Yui how to address the envelope. Yui borrowed scissors too. She cut out the cake and put it in the envelope with her letter. It was a present for her seven-year-old grandfather.

The man put the letter in a silver box. “Have you sent it already?” Yui asked. “Yes, it has already arrived,” he answered. The box was empty.

At home, her grandfather took out an old letter. “I received this from a friend in the future on my seventh birthday,” he said. Inside was a paper cake. Yui gave him a real cake too.
