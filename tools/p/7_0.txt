Title: The Grateful Crane

Long ago, a young man lived near a mountain. One winter morning, he saw a crane on the snow. A thin thread was wrapped around its leg.

The young man borrowed scissors from the old woman next door. He cut the thread with them. Then he fed the crane rice from his hand. The crane flew away.

That evening, a young woman came to his house. “May I come in? This is a thank-you gift,” she said, giving him some white cloth. The young man gave her a warm meal.

The young woman studied foreign words too. “What is ‘arigatou’ in English?” she asked him. He taught her “thank you.” She learned English from him.

The next morning, the young woman asked, “Any thread?” The young man received some from the old woman. The old woman also lent her an old weaving tool. She used it to weave cloth.

“Please do not open this room,” the young woman said. That evening, he stood outside the door and asked, “Have you finished yet?” There was no answer. He opened the door a little.

The young woman was not in the room. The crane was weaving cloth with its own feathers. “I have finished the cloth for your gift,” the crane said. “So it was you,” said the young man.

“What is ‘sayounara’ in English?” asked the crane. He taught it “goodbye,” but tears came to his eyes. The crane gave him the last piece of cloth and flew away. Every winter after that, he gave rice to the birds.
