Title: The Palace of Stars and the Young Butler

Once upon a time, there was a Palace of Stars on a mountain close to the sky. The Moon Queen lived there. The starlight in the palace never went out, even at night. Fifteen-year-old Haru began working there as a new butler.

On his first day, an older butler taught Haru an important rule. “When speaking about Her Majesty, use respectful expressions such as irasshaimasu and meshiagarimasu. For yourself, use humble expressions such as mairimasu and itadakimasu.” Haru practiced many times. But there were so many difficult words that his head began to spin.

The next morning, the queen came to the dining room. Haru bowed deeply. “Good morning, Your Majesty. Breakfast is ready,” he said respectfully. The queen smiled and said, “Thank you.” Haru felt very happy.

That night, the palace's great star clock stopped. As the clock stopped, the stars in the sky slowly began to grow dim. The queen said that she wished to inspect the clock. Haru respectfully replied, “I shall go and examine it first,” and hurried to the clock tower.

Inside the clock was a little star dragon. The dragon was so hungry that it could not move. Haru politely asked, “What would you like to eat?” The dragon replied, “I want some sweet star bread.” Haru ran to the kitchen.

There was only one star bread in the kitchen. It was a special loaf made for the queen. Haru returned to the queen and said, “I have a request. May I give this bread to the dragon?” The queen replied, “Of course. Please take it.”

When Haru brought the bread, the dragon ate it happily. Suddenly, the dragon's body shone gold, and the star clock began moving again. A thousand stars sparkled in the sky. The queen came to the tower and said, “Well done.” Haru bowed and thanked her.

The next day, the queen made Haru the highest-ranking butler in the palace. But Haru had one little problem. Every night, the star dragon came to his room and said, “Give me some bread!” Haru laughed and replied, “Dear guest, please wait just a little longer.” The palace was once again filled with bright stars and the wonderful smell of fresh bread.
