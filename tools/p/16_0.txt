Title: Kintaro and the Mountain Bridge

Long ago, a boy named Kintaro lived in the mountains. He was big and strong. Every morning, he washed his face, ate a hearty meal, and played with the animals.

One day, Kintaro wrestled with a bear. The bear had thick arms and short legs. “This is how to place your feet,” Kintaro said, gently pushing the bear.

After wrestling, they all went to the river. But yesterday’s rain had washed the bridge away. There was plenty of red fruit on the other bank. “How do we get across?” asked the rabbit.

Kintaro looked at the nearby trees. “Which tree would be good?” The bear pointed to a tall, thick tree. Kintaro pushed hard and toppled it across the river.

The tree bridge was ready, but the rabbit’s legs were shaking. Kintaro explained how to cross. “Don’t look down. Look at my back and follow me slowly.”

After crossing the river, everyone sat under a tree. “I’ll go and bring some water,” said Kintaro. They drank, ate the red fruit, and chatted happily.

Then a young samurai arrived. He was amazed by Kintaro’s strength. “Would you like to come to the capital with me?” “I’ll go and ask my mother,” said Kintaro, and he ran home.

His mother listened, smiled, and nodded. The next morning, the animals were waiting for Kintaro by the bridge. “Remember to walk slowly,” said the bear. Even strong Kintaro crossed the bridge slowly that day.
