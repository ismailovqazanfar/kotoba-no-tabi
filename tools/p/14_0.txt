Title: The Tongue-Cut Sparrow and the Little Chest

Once upon a time, an old man and an old woman lived near a mountain. Every morning, the old man gave a little sparrow some rice. The sparrow perched on his hand and sang happily.

One day, the sparrow ate the old woman’s nori. It was not the seaweed eaten with rice. It was paste for sticking paper together. The old woman became angry and hurt the sparrow’s tongue a little.

The sparrow returned to the mountain. The old man was very worried, so he went to look for it. In front of a bamboo gate, he called, “Excuse me, please open the gate.”

The gate opened, and there was the sparrow. “I’m all right now. Please come inside.” Inside the house, sparrows were preparing a meal. A tiny sparrow was washing vegetables too.

“Shall I help?” asked the old man. “Thank you, but please sit here.” The sparrows sang and danced. The old man clapped his hands and laughed.

When it was time to leave, the sparrow brought a large woven chest and a small one. “Please take one.” The old man chose the small chest. He opened it at home, so the old woman saw the gold inside too.

The next day, the old woman went to the mountain and received the large chest. She opened it on the road. Out came lots of frogs. “Please wait!” she cried, but the frogs went back to the mountain.

The old woman returned to the sparrow’s house and said, “I’m sorry.” Then she asked, “Shall I wash the dishes?” The sparrow smiled and handed her a plate. From that day on, she too waited for their little friend every morning.
